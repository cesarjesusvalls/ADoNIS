\section{A Differentiable Neutrino Event Generator}
\label{sec:adonis}
%% =====================================================================

We developed a JAX-based open-source neutrino event
generator, \textbf{\adonis}\footnote{\href{https://github.com/cesarjesusvalls/ADoNIS/}{https://github.com/cesarjesusvalls/ADoNIS/}},
which produces exclusive final-state neutrino--nucleus interactions whose
predicted distributions are differentiable, event by event, with respect to the
physics parameters that govern them. \adonis follows the interaction physics of
the ACHILLES generator~\cite{Isaacson:2020wlx, Isaacson:2022cwh}, from which it
inherits the nuclear ground state, the electroweak hard-scattering amplitudes,
and the intranuclear cascade; the technical details of those calculations are
given there and are not reproduced here. This section instead describes how each
step of the calculation is arranged so that gradient information is preserved all
the way from the physics parameters to the final binned observables.

As in the companion differentiable optical-detector simulator~\cite{LUCiD}, JAX
was chosen for its automatic differentiation, just-in-time compilation, and
vectorization, which together allow exact gradients to be propagated through the
full generation chain at a cost comparable to a single forward evaluation, on
either CPU or GPU. Throughout, we denote by $\bm\theta$ the vector of physics
parameters. In the present work $\bm\theta$ collects $28$ knobs spanning three
physics blocks: the electroweak vertex (the quasi-elastic and resonant axial
masses and axial strengths), the intranuclear cascade (the pion- and
nucleon-scattering rates, absorption, and charge-exchange fractions), and the
nuclear ground state (the Fermi momentum, the removal-energy offset, the
short-range-correlation tail, and an overall spectral-function normalization).
Each knob equals unity, or its physical nominal value, at the generator's default
tune.

\subsection{Differentiability by reweighting}
\label{sec:adonis_reweighting}

A neutrino event generator is an intrinsically stochastic program: it samples a
neutrino energy from a flux, an interaction channel, a struck nucleon from the
nuclear ground state, a hard-scattering kinematic configuration from a squared
amplitude, and a sequence of final-state re-interactions from the cascade.
Differentiating such a program directly is obstructed at every one of these
steps, both by the discrete choices and by the expensive, tabulated amplitude
evaluations that are not themselves written to be differentiated.

\adonis circumvents this in the same spirit that a differentiable detector
simulator circumvents differentiating \textsc{Geant4}~\cite{LUCiD}: the
well-understood physics that need not be optimized is evaluated \emph{once}, when
the events are generated, and captured in a compact per-event record;
differentiability is then arranged only over the physics parameters of interest,
through a closed-form \emph{reweighting} of that record. Concretely, \adonis
generates a fixed sample of events, a \emph{bank}, and stores with each event the
auxiliary quantities from which its weight can be recomputed as a function of
$\bm\theta$. The generation is a conventional, stochastic forward pass; the object
that is made differentiable is the per-event weight
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adonis_weight}
w_i(\bm\theta) \;=\; w_i^{0}\,
\underbrace{r^{\rm vertex}_i(\bm\theta)}_{\text{amplitude}}\,
\underbrace{r^{\rm FSI}_i(\bm\theta)}_{\text{cascade}}\,
\underbrace{r^{\rm SF}_i(\bm\theta)}_{\text{nucleus}},
\end{equation}
where $w_i^{0}$ is the fixed proposal weight assigned at generation and each
factor $r_i$ is a differentiable ratio, equal to one at the nominal tune, that
transports the event from the parameters it was generated with to the parameters
$\bm\theta$. The three factors correspond to the three physics blocks above and
are described in turn below. Because every $r_i$ is a pure JAX function of
$\bm\theta$, so is the product, and the entire map
$\bm\theta \mapsto w_i(\bm\theta)$ is available to automatic differentiation.

The remainder of this section describes how each stage of the calculation
supplies its part of Eq.~\ref{eq:adonis_weight} without breaking the gradient.

\subsection{Initial state}
\label{sec:adonis_initial}

The generation of an event begins with the selection of the incident probe and
the initial nuclear configuration: a neutrino (or, for electron-scattering
validation, an electron) energy is drawn from the beam flux, an interaction
channel is chosen, and a struck nucleon is sampled from the target nucleus.
These choices define the event and are held fixed once generated; they set the
kinematic support over which the reweighting acts, but are not themselves varied
in $\bm\theta$. The flux and channel selection therefore enter only through the
fixed proposal weight $w_i^{0}$, while the struck-nucleon draw is retained in the
event record so that the nuclear ground state can be reweighted, as described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:adonis_nucleus}. Keeping the sampling of the initial state outside
the differentiable map is deliberate: it isolates the discrete, non-differentiable
choices in the frozen generation pass, leaving the downstream weight a smooth
function of the physics parameters.

\subsection{Nuclear ground state}
\label{sec:adonis_nucleus}

The target nucleus is described by a spectral function, the probability density
for finding the struck nucleon with momentum $|\mathbf{p}|$ and removal energy
$E$~\cite{Isaacson:2020wlx}. Each generated event stores the $(|\mathbf{p}|, E)$
of its struck nucleon, and the nuclear factor in Eq.~\ref{eq:adonis_weight} is
the ratio of the spectral function under $\bm\theta$ to that under the nominal
tune,
\begin{equation}
r^{\rm SF}_i(\bm\theta) \;=\;
\frac{S_{\bm\theta}\!\left(|\mathbf{p}|_i, E_i\right)}
     {S_{\rm nom}\!\left(|\mathbf{p}|_i, E_i\right)},
\end{equation}
a density ratio that is a smooth function of the ground-state parameters: the
Fermi momentum $k_F$, an overall normalization, the strength of the
short-range-correlation high-momentum tail, and a rigid shift of the removal
energy. Because this factor is evaluated pointwise on the stored $(|\mathbf{p}|,E)$
of each event, it is differentiable with respect to all of these parameters
without re-sampling the nucleus.

The removal-energy shift illustrates a subtlety common to any physically bounded
parameter. The removal energy cannot be negative, and the spectral-function
model is flat, with an identically vanishing gradient, once the shift would push
$E$ below zero. This is a non-differentiable corner. We anchor the nominal value
on the smooth, one-sided branch of the response, a small positive offset from the
wall, so that a well-defined one-sided gradient is always available to the
optimizer; the resulting truncated, non-Gaussian posterior at the boundary is
studied explicitly in Sec.~\ref{sec:fitting}.

\subsection{Hard-scattering amplitudes}
\label{sec:adonis_amplitudes}

At the interaction vertex the event is weighted by the squared electroweak
amplitude for the selected channel: quasi-elastic scattering, governed by the
nucleon axial and vector form factors, and resonant pion production, described by
the dynamical coupled-channels amplitudes used in
ACHILLES~\cite{Isaacson:2022cwh, Nakamura:2015rta}. Evaluating these amplitudes
is the most expensive step of the calculation and, in the case of the tabulated
coupled-channels amplitudes, is not written to be differentiated. Rather than
autodifferentiate through that evaluation, \adonis captures its dependence on the
parameters of interest at generation time.

The key observation is that the squared amplitude is a low-order polynomial in
the axial \emph{strength} scale of each channel. At generation, \adonis therefore
evaluates the squared amplitude at three values of that scale and stores, per
event, the coefficients $(a_i, b_i, c_i)$ of the resulting quadratic together
with the momentum transfer $Q^2_i$. The vertex reweighting factor is then the
closed form
\begin{equation}
r^{\rm vertex}_i(\bm\theta)
= \frac{a_i + b_i\,s(\bm\theta) + c_i\,s(\bm\theta)^2}{a_i + b_i + c_i}
\;\times\; \mathcal{R}\!\left(Q^2_i;\, M_A(\bm\theta)\right),
\end{equation}
where $s(\bm\theta)$ is the axial-strength dial and $\mathcal{R}$ is the smooth
form-factor ratio that maps the generated axial mass to $M_A(\bm\theta)$, a
dipole ratio for the quasi-elastic vertex. Both pieces are elementary
differentiable functions of the vertex parameters. In this way the physics of the
amplitude, its full $Q^2$ and kinematic structure, is computed once by the
underlying generator and referenced to ACHILLES, while its dependence on the
axial masses and strengths is exposed to automatic differentiation exactly and at
negligible fit-time cost. This is the neutrino-vertex analogue of replacing an
expensive, non-differentiable calculation by a compact differentiable stand-in,
fit once and evaluated cheaply thereafter~\cite{LUCiD}.

\subsection{Intranuclear cascade}
\label{sec:adonis_cascade}

The hadrons produced at the vertex propagate through the residual nucleus and
undergo final-state interactions, modeled by the ACHILLES intranuclear
cascade~\cite{Isaacson:2020wlx}: elastic and inelastic nucleon--nucleon and
pion--nucleon scattering, pion absorption, and charge exchange. The cascade is
the most stochastic stage of the generation, a variable-length sequence of
sampled re-interactions, and is precisely the kind of discrete, path-dependent
process whose derivative is not captured by differentiating any single realized
history.

\adonis records, for each event, the cascade history actually realized, in a
compact per-event structure, so that the probability of that history can be
recomputed under modified interaction rates. The cascade factor in
Eq.~\ref{eq:adonis_weight} is the ratio of the history's probability under
$\bm\theta$ to its probability under the nominal rates,
\begin{equation}
r^{\rm FSI}_i(\bm\theta)
= \prod_{n \in \text{history}_i}
\frac{p_n(\bm\theta)}{p_n(\text{nom})},
\end{equation}
a product over the interaction and survival probabilities of each step, each a
smooth function of the corresponding rate dial (the pion and nucleon elastic and
inelastic cross sections, the absorption rate, and the charge-exchange fraction)
and equal to unity at nominal. Reweighting the stored history in this way is the
importance-weight expression of the change of rates, and it is differentiable in
the cascade parameters without re-running the cascade. In the discrete-choice
setting this plays the role that score-function gradient estimators play for
stochastic detector simulators~\cite{LUCiD}: the gradient of an expectation over
sampled paths is carried by the derivative of the log-probability of the realized
choices, here evaluated analytically from the stored history.

\subsection{Differentiable events and observables}
\label{sec:adonis_events}

The result of the generation is a bank in which every event carries, alongside
its final-state kinematics, the records of
Secs.~\ref{sec:adonis_nucleus}--\ref{sec:adonis_cascade} that render its weight
$w_i(\bm\theta)$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:adonis_weight} an exact, differentiable function
of the physics parameters. Differentiability is thus a property of each stored
event individually: for any event one can ask, and answer exactly, how its weight
responds to any physics parameter.

This event-by-event differentiability is what makes the downstream analysis
differentiable for free. Physics observables are built from the events by
operations that do not depend on $\bm\theta$: a signal or topology definition
selects events by their final state, a choice of kinematic variable maps each
event to an axis value, and a binning assigns each event to a bin. None of these
touches the weights, so a binned prediction is simply the sum of the differentiable
per-event weights falling in each bin,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adonis_binned}
m_b(\bm\theta) = \sum_{i \in b} w_i(\bm\theta),
\qquad
\frac{\partial m_b}{\partial\bm\theta} = \sum_{i \in b}
\frac{\partial w_i}{\partial\bm\theta}.
\end{equation}
The gradient of any histogram, and therefore of any downstream statistic built
from it, is obtained by propagating the gradients of the weights through the same
selection and binning. Changing the signal definition, the observable, or the bin
edges changes which events enter each sum but not the differentiability of the
result. An analysis is free to define its observables however it wishes and
retains exact gradients with respect to the physics parameters throughout.

We note the one boundary in this construction. The per-event weight
$w_i(\bm\theta)$ is a pure JAX quantity and is the object automatic
differentiation traces; the histogramming of Eq.~\ref{eq:adonis_binned} is a
scatter-add of those weights into bins, which consumes an already-differentiated
quantity and carries its gradient through unchanged. \adonis differentiates the
map from physics parameters to per-event weights and, through it, to observables;
it does not differentiate the frozen generation pass that produced the sample, by
design, just as a differentiable detector simulator need not differentiate the
\textsc{Geant4} photon sample underlying its emission surrogate~\cite{LUCiD}.

\subsection{Gradients and higher-order derivatives}
\label{sec:adonis_gradients}

Because $\bm\theta \mapsto \{w_i\}$ is a JAX function, the Jacobian of the binned
prediction,
\begin{equation}
J_{bk} = \frac{\partial m_b}{\partial\theta_k},
\end{equation}
is obtained by automatic differentiation. As the map takes few inputs (the
parameter vector) to many outputs (the per-event weights, reduced to bins), we
evaluate it in forward mode: a single Jacobian-vector product with a unit tangent
$\mathbf{e}_k$ returns the full per-event derivative in the $k$-th parameter
direction in one forward pass, and the columns for all parameters are batched into
one vectorized evaluation whose cost is a small multiple of a single forward
reweight. This replaces the finite differences a conventional generator would
require, with their noise and step-size bias, and mirrors the forward-mode Fisher
assembly used for gradient-based detector calibration~\cite{LUCiD}.

The same construction extends to higher order. A directional second derivative is
a nested Jacobian-vector product, a Jacobian-vector product of a
Jacobian-vector product, and a mixed $k$-th derivative is a $k$-fold nesting along
$k$ tangent directions; \adonis assembles these into the second- and
third-derivative tensors of the prediction by automatic differentiation alone,
with no symbolic derivative code. From the first derivatives we form the
Gauss--Newton, or Fisher, matrix
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adonis_fisher}
F_{kl} = \sum_b \frac{1}{\sigma_b^{2}}\,
\frac{\partial m_b}{\partial\theta_k}\,
\frac{\partial m_b}{\partial\theta_l},
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_b$ the per-bin uncertainty, which is the positive-semidefinite
curvature underlying the fits of Sec.~\ref{sec:fitting}, the same second-order
object that drives gradient-based detector fits~\cite{LUCiD}. The higher tensors
supply the non-Gaussian corrections to the likelihood used there.

Two implementation choices make this practical. First, the expensive amplitude and
cascade physics is amortized into the bank at generation time
(Secs.~\ref{sec:adonis_amplitudes}--\ref{sec:adonis_cascade}), so the fit-time
reweight evaluates only elementary closed forms and is inexpensive to compile and
differentiate at every iteration. Second, the generation and reweighting are
performed in double precision, as the perturbative reweighting factors of
Eq.~\ref{eq:adonis_weight} lie too close to unity to resolve in single precision.
The resulting cost of a full gradient is a small, parameter-count-independent
multiple of one forward reweight.

\noindent\textbf{Method (shared by every Sec-3/Sec-2 figure).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item One per-bin Jacobian $J$ (bins $\times$ 28 knobs), assembled on carbon by
        \texttt{sec3\_gradients/build\_multisample.py::build()}, concatenating persisted per-sample
        Jacobians (T2K CC0/1$\pi$ STV$+$muon, MINERvA STV, MINERvA $p_T/p_\parallel$, $(e,e')$) $+$ 3
        FSI-only hadron-beam Jacobians ($\pi^+/p/n$--C, \texttt{beams/beam\_fisher.py}). Every $J_{ik}$ is
        an \emph{exact autodiff} $\partial(\mathrm{bin}\,i)/\partial\theta_k$ via one \texttt{jax.jvp} per
        knob --- not finite differences. Output: \texttt{output/altgen/multisample\_carbon.npz}.
  \item Error model: diagonal $\sigma=\sqrt{(\mathrm{syst}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{central})^2+\mathrm{mcerr}^2}$,
        empty-bin guard $\sigma=\infty$ (\texttt{fisher\_engine.py:19-28}).
  \item Fisher: $F=(J/\sigma)^\top(J/\sigma)$ (Asimov, additive across samples/bins). Prior$+$posterior:
        $V=(F+\mathrm{diag}(1/\mathrm{prior}^2))^{-1}$.
  \item \textbf{Shrinkage} (Gate I): $\mathrm{shrink}=\sqrt{\mathrm{diag}(V)}/\mathrm{prior}$; knob is FIT
        when $\mathrm{shrink}<0.5$. \textbf{Reach}: $\mathrm{reach}=\sqrt{\mathrm{diag}(F)}$, raw shrinkage
        $=1/(\mathrm{reach}\cdot\mathrm{prior})$ (other knobs fixed). Raw vs.\ marginalized separates:
        MEASURABLE (both $<$cut), DEGENERATE (raw$<$, marg$>$), INVISIBLE (raw$>$cut).
  \item Degeneracy: eigendecompose prior-scaled $F_s=F\odot\mathrm{prior}\otimes\mathrm{prior}$;
        $\lambda>1=$ data-dominated modes. Column membership declared in \texttt{configs/paper/sec2\_subsets.yaml}
        (row-slices of the one persisted npz, no re-run). Driver:
        \texttt{python -m analysis.paper.sec2\_fisher.make}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Method.} Sec 2 and Sec 3 consume the \emph{same} per-bin Jacobian (\texttt{multisample\_carbon.npz}),
computed once by autodiff, read two ways: Fisher/constrainability (Sec 2) vs.\ raw gradient reach $+$ shape
(Sec 3). Per-knob directional derivative $=$ single forward-mode \texttt{jax.jvp} through the frozen

\subsection{Methods}

\paragraph{M1 --- multisample closure setup (\texttt{physfit/multisample.py}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item All fits in the same 28-knob \texttt{physical\_fit} basis (\texttt{SPEC/NPAR/PNAMES/PRIOR},
        sourced from \texttt{reweight\_model.knob\_specs}). Priors 20\% multiplicative default, with
        \texttt{Eb\_shift} $\pm4$~MeV and \texttt{f\_NN\_cex} $\pm0.10$ (\texttt{\_PRIOR\_POLICY}).
  \item \textbf{Gate-I dial subset (16 of 28)}: knobs with combined marginalized shrinkage $<0.5$ from the
        Asimov Fisher study (\texttt{multisample\_carbon.npz}); \texttt{np.where(g["shrink"]<0.5)}
        (\texttt{multisample.py:274}). Same subset as Sec 2/3 --- Sec 4 closes the loop with a genuine
        nonlinear fit instead of the linear Fisher toy.
  \item 20 sample blocks stacked in one fixed order (asserted against \texttt{multisample\_carbon.npz}):
        T2K CC0/1$\pi$ STV$+$muon (7), MINERvA STV (3) $+$ qelike $p_T/p_\parallel$ (2), $(e,e')$ QE/RES
        $\omega$ (2), $\pi^+/p/n\!\rightarrow\!$C tagged beams (2 each). \texttt{MultiEngine}
        (\texttt{multisample.py:188-227}) duck-types the single-bank \texttt{Engine} over
        \texttt{BankSample} $+$ \texttt{BeamSample}; both reweights pure JAX $\Rightarrow$ exact 1st/2nd/3rd
        directional derivatives via nested \texttt{jax.jvp}.
  \item \textbf{Closure data}: \texttt{set\_closure\_data(truth)} sets \texttt{d["data"]} $=$ the EXACT
        nonlinear reweight at an injected truth (not a linearization); refresh $\sigma$ via
        \texttt{bin\_sigma} (SYST $=5\%$). Headline ``random'' truth: each dial displaced by
        $U(0.5,2)\cdot\mathrm{prior}$ with random sign (\texttt{Eb\_shift} forced positive).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{M2 --- MLE/MAP fit and Newton-decrement convergence (\texttt{physical\_fit\_run.py:242-303}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{lm\_fit} minimizes $\chi^2_\mathrm{total}=\chi^2_\mathrm{data}+\chi^2_\mathrm{prior}$ by
        LM/Gauss--Newton over the 16-dial subset; prior for FITTED knobs only, always centered at
        \texttt{eng.th0}.
  \item \textbf{Stopping rule: Newton decrement} $nd=g^\top A^{-1}g$ (\texttt{physical\_fit\_run.py:278}),
        $A=J^\top W J+\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{prior}_w)$. $nd$ is the predicted objective gap to the
        quadratic minimum and is landscape-invariant: certifies the argmin even on flat/near-degenerate
        directions, where a relative-$\chi^2$ test can stall. Converge when $nd<\mathrm{tol}$ ($10^{-6}$)
        or step-norm underflow.
  \item Full GN steps (\texttt{PHYSFIT\_STEP\_SCALE=1.0} default) $\Rightarrow$ quadratic convergence
        near the minimum; \texttt{STEP\_SCALE}$<1$ only to force a slow, visualizable trajectory demo.
  \item \texttt{Eb\_shift} boundary: \texttt{sf\_reweight} clamps $E_b<0\rightarrow0$ (model exactly flat,
        zero gradient below the wall --- an absorbing LM trap); every trial step projects
        $\texttt{Eb\_shift}=\max(\texttt{Eb\_shift},10^{-2})$ to keep the one-sided gradient alive.
  \item Covariance: $V=\mathrm{pinv}(A,\mathrm{rcond}=10^{-12})$ --- the GN/Laplace (parabolic) posterior
        curvature; $\sigma_\mathrm{post}=\sqrt{\mathrm{diag}(V)}$ is every ``Gaussian $\sigma$'' bar in Sec 4.
  \item MLE vs MAP: \texttt{S4\_PRIOR\_SCALE} scales the fit's prior width after truth injection.
        $=0\Rightarrow$ prior$\times10^6\Rightarrow$ MLE (unregularized); $=1.0\Rightarrow$ MAP. On a
        noiseless closure the MLE lands exactly on truth ($\chi^2_\mathrm{data}=0$); the MAP is prior-pulled
        toward nominal by an amount tracking the dial's Gate-I shrinkage.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{M3 --- exact profile-likelihood posterior (\texttt{multisample\_profile.py}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Per fitted dial $k$: scan $\theta_k$ on $\hat\theta_k\pm3\sigma_{\mathrm{post},k}$ ($S4\_PROFILE\_N$
        nodes/side, default 6 $\rightarrow$ 13), at each node FIX $\theta_k$ and re-minimize the other 15
        dials by \texttt{lm\_fit} --- a genuine profile likelihood, not a linearization.
        $\Delta_\mathrm{profile}(\theta_k)=\mathrm{obj}(\theta_k,\hat\theta_{-k})-\mathrm{obj}(\mathrm{BFP})$.
  \item Marginal density $\propto\exp(-\tfrac12\Delta_\mathrm{profile})$ (profile-as-marginal/Laplace).
        Diagnostic: compare to the GN parabola $((\theta_k-\hat\theta_k)/\sigma_{\mathrm{post},k})^2$ ---
        agreement over $\pm3\sigma$ means the quoted quadratic error IS the likelihood.
  \item 68\% credible interval: cubic-spline the profile, density $\propto\exp(-\Delta\chi^2/2)$, normalize,
        take the highest-density region containing 68.27\% --- NOT symmetric $\pm1\sigma$; lets the interval
        go one-sided/asymmetric at a wall.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{M4 --- autodiff Taylor corner (\texttt{multisample\_derivs.py} $+$ \texttt{corner\_taylor.py}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item At the noiseless MLE minimum (zero residual), expand the model to 2nd order:
        $\chi^2(D)=\sum_b W_b((J\!\cdot\!D)_b+\tfrac12(D\!\cdot\!d^2m\!\cdot\!D)_b+\dots)^2$. Gaussian at
        leading order, non-Gaussianity carried by $d^2m$; sum of squares $\Rightarrow\ge0$, never diverges.
  \item Tensors computed once at the BFP by nested \texttt{jax.jvp} (no \texttt{jax.jet}): $J_b$,
        $B_b$ (via polarization $B_{ij}=\tfrac12(d^2(e_i+e_j)-d^2e_i-d^2e_j)$), optional $C_b$ (3rd mixed,
        gated \texttt{S4\_ORDER}$\ge3$, symmetrized).
  \item \textbf{Model order $N\Rightarrow\chi^2$ exact to order $N{+}1$}: 2nd-order model ($J_b,B_b$)
        $\Rightarrow\chi^2$ exact to 3rd order; adding $C_b\Rightarrow$ exact to 4th order.
  \item \texttt{corner\_taylor.profile\_pair} profiles this polynomial $\chi^2$ over an $(a,b)$ grid by a
        cheap inner GN over the other 14 dials directly on the polynomial (no bank re-eval) --- orders of
        magnitude cheaper than re-fitting the full nonlinear model per node.
  \item Exact numerical corner (\texttt{multisample\_corner.py}) avoids 16-D importance-sampling
        degeneracy (ESS $\sim$1\%): sets the other 14 dials to their Gaussian-conditional mean given
        $(a,b)$ ($M=V_{o,ab}\,\mathrm{pinv}(V_{ab,ab})$), then evaluates the true nonlinear $\chi^2$ ---
        exact to leading order in the nuisance shift, one model eval per node.
\end{itemize}
